06Related work & background

The field this project builds on.

Quantum-safe algorithms are standardised and the first chips that implement them are shipping. What is missing is a rigorous way to test that silicon for side-channel leakage. Below: how the field arrived here, the chips now in scope, and the literature we build on.

Key terms, in plain language
Post-quantum cryptography (PQC)
New encryption methods designed to stay secure even against a future quantum computer.
Quantum-safe chip
A hardware chip that runs post-quantum encryption to protect a device's secrets.
Side-channel attack
Stealing secrets not by breaking the maths, but by measuring a chip's physical signals — power use, electromagnetic waves or timing.
Harvest now, decrypt later
Capturing encrypted data today and storing it to unscramble once quantum computers are strong enough.
Cryptographic key
The secret that locks and unlocks encrypted data. If it leaks, the protection is gone.
ML-KEM / ML-DSA
The first encryption and digital-signature standards chosen by NIST for the quantum era (formerly Kyber and Dilithium).

How the field got here

  1. 1994

    Shor's algorithm shows a quantum computer could break RSA and elliptic-curve cryptography

  2. 2016

    NIST opens the Post-Quantum Cryptography standardisation process

  3. May 2019

    Gidney & Ekerå estimate ~20 million noisy qubits and 8 hours to break RSA-2048

  4. Jul 2022

    First algorithms selected: Kyber, Dilithium, Falcon and SPHINCS+

  5. Aug 2022

    SIKE, a NIST finalist, is broken on a single classical PC; NIST drops it

  6. Sep 2023

    Signal ships PQXDH — post-quantum key agreement at billion-user scale

  7. Feb 2024

    Apple brings PQ3 quantum-secure encryption to iMessage

  8. Aug 2024

    NIST finalises FIPS 203 (ML-KEM), 204 (ML-DSA) and 205 (SLH-DSA)

  9. Nov 2024

    Chrome enables hybrid ML-KEM in TLS for all desktop users

  10. Dec 2024

    Google's Willow chip demonstrates below-threshold quantum error correction

  11. Mar 2025

    NIST selects HQC as a backup, code-based key-encapsulation mechanism

  12. May 2025

    Gidney re-estimates the RSA-2048 break at under 1 million qubits — a ~20× drop

  13. Sep 2026

    FIPS 140-2 sunset — cryptographic modules validate under FIPS 140-3

  14. 2027 → 2035

    NSA CNSA 2.0 window: PQC required for new systems by 2027, full migration by 2035

Open directions

  • A standardised, reproducible side-channel test methodology for PQC hardware — the gap this project targets.
  • Masking and constant-time implementations at acceptable area and energy cost.
  • Crypto-agility in silicon: swapping algorithms without re-spinning hardware if a scheme is broken.
